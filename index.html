Overview

World models have enabled learning from large-scale, unstructured visual data, driving unprecedented performance in prediction and decision-making. Translating these advances into real-world robot deployment requires fast, reactive policies that can respond reliably to changing environments. From dexterous manipulation to drone racing, robots must combine an understanding of geometry and dynamics with the ability to act under tight latency and computational constraints. This motivates a central challenge: how can we retain the benefits of scalable world modeling while enabling real-time perception, prediction, and control?

This workshop provides a platform to discuss research progress in building real-time world models for scalable embodied intelligence. We bring together researchers in world modeling, robot learning, and efficient machine learning to explore representations, learning methods, and architectures that connect rich predictive capabilities with responsive behavior. Through contributed research and discussion, we aim to identify shared challenges and promising directions for turning knowledge learned from diverse data into effective action in the physical world.

Call for Papers

Draft

Proposed call; submissions are not yet open.

We invite research on world models that support real-time perception, prediction, and control in robotics. We welcome new methods, empirical studies, and work in progress, including negative results that clarify the trade-offs between predictive accuracy, latency, and computational cost.

Topics of interest

  • Latent world models: Predictive representation learning and latent-space modeling, including JEPA-style approaches.
  • Efficient reconstruction objectives: Alternative prediction and reconstruction targets that reduce the computational cost of world modeling.
  • Model compression and acceleration: Making large world models smaller and faster for real-time deployment.
  • Training under deployment constraints: Training strategies and scaling laws that account for inference-time limits on compute, memory, and latency.
  • World models for compact state spaces: Applications such as drone control where low-dimensional state prediction can replace full video modeling.
  • Integrating prediction and control: Combining slow, rich predictions with fast world-model updates and feedback in a real-time control loop.

Submission format

Up to 4 pages of main text, excluding references, in ICLR format. Submissions should be anonymized for double-blind review. The workshop is non-archival.

We prioritize new and ongoing work. Papers already published at a peer-reviewed conference are not eligible; preprints are welcome. Reviews will consider relevance, technical soundness, and the insight offered, including evidence for real-time performance where applicable.

Accepted papers will be presented as posters, with a subset selected for spotlight presentations.
